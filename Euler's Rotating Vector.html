Euler's formula as a rotating vector

eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ isn't an algebra trick — it's a compact way to describe a vector of length 1 spinning around the origin. Watch the vector turn, and watch its horizontal shadow trace out a cosine wave.

Beyond the unit circle

The general rotating phasor: A·ei(ωt + φ)

In audio, a signal is rarely just cos θ — it has a size, a speed of oscillation, and a starting point. These three sliders map directly onto that same rotating-vector picture.

Amplitude= radius of the vector.
Frequency= how fast it spins.
Phase= where it starts spinning from.
A·ei(ωt+φ), with A = 1.00, f = 0.25 Hz, φ = 0.00 rad
Real part → A·cos(ωt+φ) = 1.00
This is exactly the signal you'd see on an oscilloscope: the shadow of a spinning vector.
